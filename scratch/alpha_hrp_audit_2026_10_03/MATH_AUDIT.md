# PatchTST math and original five-task audit — October 3, 2026

The original five-task training had a confirmed loss-scaling bug: the code described the Hugging Face loss as equally weighted **normalized** MSE, but the library actually evaluates **denormalized** MSE. Volume contributes about 99% of the squared error in the reproduced batch. There was also a confirmed configuration bug: the declared `stride=8` was ignored by the adapter, making the historical model use stride 1 and 45 patches. Neither is a newly introduced reversed-ranking bug in the current model.

The most informative ML finding is that the May checkpoint actually used during the strong May/June period produces nearly constant predictions in normalized space. Its close forecasts are therefore almost entirely the rolling-mean term restored by RevIN. Its cross-sectional ranking correlates **0.9999+** with 60-session close momentum in the reproduced original weeks. The current model adds a much larger learned adjustment, changing roughly 6–7 of the 15 top-ranked names. That adjustment has no positive measured rank skill over the six recent weeks. This explains what changed in the forecasting function; it does **not** establish the channel-count change alone as the cause of the account's losses.

My earlier August 14 comparison was narrower than the user intended: that five-input checkpoint already optimized close-only loss. This audit explicitly includes the original May and July **five-task** checkpoints.

## Confirmed old loss bug: RevIN did not balance the five tasks

Historical source at `07d117a^`, `core/patchtst/training.py`, says the built-in loss operates in normalized space and is equal-weight multi-task learning. The actual installed HF implementation computes:

```text
raw_prediction = normalized_prediction * scale + loc
loss = mean((raw_prediction - raw_future_target)^2)
```

In the July 24 checkpoint's 64-stock August 24 probe:

| Channel | Raw MSE |
|---|---:|
| Open | 0.00049073 |
| High | 0.00043900 |
| Low | 0.00043204 |
| Close | 0.00052132 |
| Volume | 0.17415996 |

Volume accounts for **98.93%** of the sum. HF loss is 0.03520861, matching manual raw MSE. Manual normalized MSE is 0.67967904 and plainly different. Thus the original checkpoint-selection loss mostly evaluated volume, not close forecasts. This bug predates the recent decline; its existence is not evidence that restoring the old loss will improve price selection.

HF's denormalization and MSE are visible in its [v4.57.3 source](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v4.57.3/src/transformers/models/patchtst/modeling_patchtst.py). The repository's installed version was inspected directly as well.

## RevIN creates a mathematically forced momentum term

For one close-return context, let `mu` be its 60-session mean and `s = sqrt(variance + 1e-5)`. The encoder receives `z = (x - mu) / s`. Its raw five-session log-return forecast is:

```text
weekly_log_forecast = 5 * mu + s * sum(network(z)[0:5])
weekly_return_pct = 100 * (exp(weekly_log_forecast) - 1)
```

Adding a constant `delta` to every historical close log return leaves `z` unchanged. The model must therefore add `5 * delta` to its weekly log forecast, regardless of its weights. A +0.001 daily shift multiplies predicted weekly gross return by `exp(0.005) = 1.00501252`. The numerical probes agree to less than 0.00000001 daily log-return error.

This is an architectural assumption when forecasting returns: the encoder cannot directly see the removed mean and cannot freely learn how much of that mean should carry into the future. It is shared by old and current models, so it cannot by itself explain an August-only regression. It is a stronger explanation of the observed momentum-like ranking than assuming a numerical percent/log-return conversion error.

## Five channels still affect training, even with close-only loss

HF is channel independent with shared weights and channel attention disabled in these artifacts. Converting a five-channel checkpoint to one channel while freezing its geometry and all weights reproduces its eval-mode close forecasts within approximately **0.000000007** daily log-return units. Reversing the four non-close input sequences changes eval-mode close output by exactly zero. This rules out a current inference close-column mix-up in that controlled comparison.

Training is different. BatchNorm pools the batch/channel dimensions. On disposable July 24 model copies, with dropout disabled to isolate normalization:

- Reversing only non-close histories changes train-mode close predictions by up to **0.01493 daily log-return units**.
- Close-only loss has a nonzero gradient into non-close inputs: norm **0.0001649**.
- Five versus one input channel, same weights and geometry, changes train-mode close output by up to **0.03500**.

These are mathematical dependence probes, not trained-policy performance estimates. They demonstrate that “four extra inputs receive no close gradient” is false in train mode with this BatchNorm architecture. Removing those channels changes training normalization in addition to removing their original auxiliary losses.

## Daily MSE and weekly ranking are different objectives

Close-only loss correctly stops optimizing volume, but daily close MSE is not mathematically equivalent to weekly return ranking. For daily errors `e_h`:

```text
daily_MSE = mean(e_h^2)
weekly_log_error_squared = (sum(e_h))^2
```

The latter contains cross terms. Daily errors can cancel in a correct weekly forecast, and a lower daily MSE can still give worse weekly ranking. Current validation rank-IC checkpointing partially addresses this mismatch; the optimized gradient is still daily MSE. This is a modeling issue, not evidence of an incorrectly coded rank calculation.

The score conversion is correct: summing daily log returns and applying `expm1` gives the compounded return. `expm1` is monotone, so it preserves ranking. Descending score sort, deterministic ties, and rank-band retention were checked. No current sign reversal, accidental volume ranking, extra inverse scaling, or probability-to-price conversion bug was found.

This mismatch remains unresolved; hypothetical target performance must not be presented as fully reconciled broker PnL or used to assign all account loss to the model.

HRP's 15-asset covariance/clustering/bisection path was reviewed. It allocates by risk after alpha screening; it does not weight by forecast strength. Low-volatility selections can therefore receive large weights even with weak forecast evidence. This behavior is consistent with the strategy's specified math. No newly introduced 15-asset weight-sign or percentage-unit error was established.

## Conclusion, correction state and evidence challenge

**Established:** historical normalized-loss assumption was wrong; historical effective stride differed from declared stride; old time-validation/metric reporting was misleading; the strong May/June model was nearly a rolling-mean momentum forecast; close-only retraining materially changed the learned ranking adjustment; and narrowing the hold band changed selection behavior before the channel migration.

**Current weak point:** low predictive evidence for the learned adjustment and for the resulting cross-sectional forecasts. Current artifact validation rank IC is 0.02338 and reported close MSE is 2.34% worse than the hindsight validation-target-mean baseline. The configured health policy checks valid artifacts/finite positive losses, not outperformance; this audit does not silently redefine it.

**Confidence limit / equivalent RiskCritic veto:** do not label “removing four channels caused the losses” as proven. Channel count, loss, BatchNorm population, geometry, checkpoint rule, hold band and model weights changed, and losses were already substantial in July. Original five-task checkpoints have negative recent forward rank correlation too. Six weeks and retrospective prices cannot establish a profitable replacement or a unique causal explanation.

The safe corrections already made locally preserve actual artifact math and enforce complete close histories. A new forecasting representation that can condition on the removed mean/scale, or a different return normalization/loss, would require a separate candidate retrain and genuine forward evaluation. Restoring the old volume-dominated loss or disabling normalization without such evidence is not a verified correction. No objective/hyperparameter/model-pointer change, training, order submission, deployment or service restart was performed in this expanded audit.
